USDA & FAO Agricultural Extension Service Bulletin #402
Title: Organic & Regenerative Management of Tomato Fungal Leaf Blights

1. Disease Cycle & Early Detection:
Early blight (Alternaria solani) and Septoria leaf spot are primary fungal pathogens in warm, humid climates. Spores overwinter in plant debris and splash onto lower leaves during rainfall or overhead irrigation.

2. Emergency Bio-Inputs & Organic Treatments:
- Neem Seed Kernel Extract (NSKE 5%): Spray on foliage every 7-10 days to inhibit fungal spore germination and deter sucking pests.
- Trichoderma viride / harzianum: Bio-fungicide applied as soil drench (5g/L water) or foliar spray to outcompete soil-borne pathogens.
- Panchagavya / Fermented Compost Tea: Diluted 3% solution acts as a foliar nutrient spray and systemic plant immunity booster.

3. Cultural & Agronomic Management:
- Mulching: Apply 2-3 inches of dry paddy straw or sugarcane bagasse around the base of tomato plants to prevent rainwater soil-splash.
- Pruning: Remove infected lower leaves up to 30 cm from ground level to improve air circulation.
- Companion Planting: Intercrop with African Marigold (Tagetes erecta) to suppress root-knot nematodes and repelling whiteflies.
